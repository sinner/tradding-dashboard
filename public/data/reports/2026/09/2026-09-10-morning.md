## The flush that wasn't a floor

At 12:47:14 UTC the feed shows 5,771,456 USD of long liquidations across 100 fills, inside a nine-second window, with exactly zero short liquidations against them. Yesterday's flush was 16.81-to-1 against longs and got called a massacre. This one has no denominator at all. 95.4 percent of it sits in the single 76,700 bucket, 36.5 points below spot. Price is standing on it.

One caveat on the magnitude, and it is important. The endpoint caps at 100 fills, and those 100 fills span nine seconds. So 5,771,456 USD is a *floor* on a nine-second window, not a session total. The true figure is not measurable from this feed and no attempt has been made to guess it.

Here is what is measurable, and it reverses endday's read entirely. Endday found open interest down 4.06 percent across yesterday and funding down 41.25 percent, and called it a genuine deleveraging. Overnight the crowd put all of it back on. Open interest is 2,169,286,257 USD, **up 4.15 percent**. Funding is +0.0001, **up 117.2 percent** — longs are paying more than double what they paid last night. And the hourly long/short account ratio has climbed through 1.32, 1.37, 1.40, 1.41 to **1.42**, the highest reading this dashboard has ever recorded, set while price was making a 24-hour low.

A flush that removes leverage is a floor. A flush that gets re-levered inside the hour is a staging area. Forced selling tells you leverage died; open interest tells you whether it was replaced. Only the first is a bottom.
