**Bias: range, bearish tilt · Confidence 5.4 / 10 · Mark 78,035.1 at 19:37 COT**

## Bias review

The midday session published outright bullish, 42 percent bull against 21 bear, the most directional call in six sessions. Price is lower. The headline call was wrong.

The gate design was not. Midday set its add trigger at 78,993.9, the twenty-four-hour high itself rather than somewhere underneath it, on the reasoning that the advance had been paid for by short covering and only a close through the high would prove real bid had arrived. That gate fired at 18:00Z with a 79,222.1 close, clearing by 228.2 points, and held for two more closes at 78,996.7 and 79,101.8 on the way to 79,600. Seven hundred and forty-six points of follow-through above the midday mark. The gate asked the right question and got a yes.

Then the answer changed. Five consecutive lower hourly closes — 79,101.8, 78,767.9, 78,557.6, 78,195.7, and the live bar at 77,981.9 — gave back 1,564.9 points from the high. The 78,029.5 reduce level midday named has been breached on price and not on a settled close; the lowest settled close since the midday mark is 78,195.7, still 166.2 points above it. That is the exact pattern that cost the desk money on 13 September: a gate unfired on closes and broken on price.

The discretionary call fared worse than the gates. Midday added a 15.00 USDT spot tranche at 78,853.6. It marks 78,035.1, about 0.16 USDT offside, and the whole 177.10 USDT book is now within five cents of its cost basis. What makes this worth writing down is that midday named the reason it would fail, in its own liquidity note, before it placed the trade. It observed that price rising while open interest falls is shorts closing rather than longs opening. It called the 78,900 and 79,000 clusters spent fuel. It cited the 13 September loss by name. Then it added anyway, because the gate had fired. Three times in four days this desk has written the correct counterweight into the report and sized against it in the wallet.
