One thing midnight got exactly right deserves credit, because it is the read that explains this whole session. It measured a 90 percent *short*-side squeeze at 02:00 and refused to call it bullish, noting that open interest rose through it and the long/short ratio climbed from 1.55 to 1.59 — shorts had been liquidated and replaced by more longs. Five hours later those longs were hunted.

## The trade

Flat futures, and that stays flat — there is no leveraged edge into a print, and midnight's hand-off said so explicitly. Spot core held.

Two settled-close gates for midday, and settled means a 1H **close**, not a touch.

**Reduce below 76,750.0.** The session low, printed into the cascade, 241.0 points under spot. Probabilities: 30 bullish, 36 range, 34 bearish. That is close to a coin flip and it is meant to be — constructive de-leveraging against a deteriorating flow backdrop is exactly what a coin flip looks like.

## Key risks

**CPI at 07:30 COT is the whole session.** A hot print puts 76,750.0 and then the emptied shelf beneath it in play immediately; a soft one has 77,498.8 as the first honest test. The desk cannot handicap it and is not pretending to.

**The emptied shelf is the specific danger.** Below 76,750.0 there is no measured resting bid down to 76,546.0, because this morning's flush consumed it. Air, not support.

**A fourth negative ETF day** would take the streak past minus 500 million and would make today's long flush look like the first leg rather than a capitulation.

**The grazed gate cuts both ways.** A reduce trigger that missed by 3.5 points is a coiled spring, and midday should treat it as live information even though this session correctly declined to act on it.

**Brent minus 3.38 percent is not unambiguously good.** Energy falling that hard in one session is as often a growth signal as a disinflation one, and the equity tape — VOO at the low of its month — is leaning toward the former reading.
