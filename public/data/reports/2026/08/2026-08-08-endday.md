## Bias review

The midday called range with a bullish tilt at 46/40/14 from 65,125.0 and moved the desk to ADD on a settled-bar trigger. Three of its four decisions held up. The reduce line at 64,650 was never threatened — the lowest trade since is 64,937.1, leaving 287.1 points of clearance. Raising the add trigger from 65,120 to 65,190 was right, and looks better with every hour: price topped at 65,125.1, sixty-five points short, so leaving the trigger where it was would have re-fired the desk into a level that was already failing.

Acting on the breakout was the one that did not work. The 14:00Z hourly bar settled at 65,128.1, above the published 65,120, and the desk bought 8 USDT of spot. Every hourly bar since has closed back underneath: 65,082.9, 65,078.3, 65,117.3, 65,046.4, 65,074.3, 65,081.6, 65,025.2, 64,945.6, 64,956.9. Eight settled closes, no reclaim.

The rule that produced the trade — require a settled bar — was not the problem. The rule the desk did not have was a confirmation rule. Nothing in the published method asked for a second settled close before committing. The midday itself wrote that the two bars after the fire had closed back under the level and called the break unconfirmed, then bought anyway on the strength of the oscillators. The tell was on the page at the time: open interest fell 0.87% while price rose, which means the breakout was paid for by shorts covering, not by new longs arriving. A breakout with no new open interest behind it has nothing to sustain it once the covering stops. On a thin weekend tape a single settled close through a level is an alert, not a confirmation, and the confirmation has to come from participation rather than from momentum.

## Key risks

The most likely way this report is wrong is that it is late. Price is 32.5 points above the session low and 33.5 above a shelf that already held once. Reading a breakdown at the bounce level is the mirror image of the midday's mistake of reading a breakout at the resistance level, and a single reclaim of 65,003.86 would invalidate most of the momentum case in an hour.

The second risk is that the sample is small. $88,280 of realized liquidations and $16,096 in the freshest cluster are not large numbers. The direction of the flow flipped, but on a tape trading 22 BTC an hour, four fills can be one participant.

The third is the gap itself, working the other way. Thin liquidity cuts both ways, and the same absence of an ETF bid that makes a downside flush easy also means a modest bid can lift price through the empty 65,190–65,388 zone with nothing structural in between.
