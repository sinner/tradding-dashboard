## Bias review

Morning called it a range with a 40 percent weight on nothing happening, and placed a reduce gate at a settled 1H close below 79,400.0. That gate fired twice.

Trigger one came at the 13:00Z close of 79,318.2 and did not survive the reclaim clause — the very next bar closed 79,544.3, a clean 144.3 points back above the line, which under the two-bar rule voids it. On the log's running tally that would have been the third whipsaw in this window. Trigger two came at the 15:00Z close of 78,329.9, 1,070.1 points through the line, and confirmed immediately when the 16:00Z bar closed 77,785.9. Against the current 77,860.2, acting on trigger one is 1,458.0 points better off and acting on trigger two is 469.7 points better off, so `acting_helped` is true on either reading.

The add gate at 79,880.0 never fired and was never close: the best settled close after publication was 79,544.3, and even the highest print of 79,839.3 fell 40.7 points short. The close-only rule cost nothing here.

The finding worth carrying forward is about the whipsaw count itself. Two of the three apparent whipsaws in this log were followed within two hours by a second trigger in the same direction that ran hard. A voided first trigger is not evidence the level was wrong — it is evidence the first probe was early. Gates should be graded on the sequence of triggers, not on the first one alone.
