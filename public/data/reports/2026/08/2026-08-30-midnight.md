## Bias review

The last session written was 28 August midday. The 28 August endday and all four 29 August sessions did not run, so this grades a 43-hour gap in one step rather than the usual overnight hop. Both gates from that session resolved inside the unmonitored window, which is why they went ungraded until now.

The REDUCE gate at a settled 1H close below 77,480.0 triggered twice and was voided twice. Trigger one closed 77,385.3 and was reclaimed on the second bar. Trigger two closed 77,475.0 — five points through the line — and was reclaimed on the very next bar. Selling either would have been about 1,250 points worse than where price trades now. The two-bar reclaim rule did exactly the job it exists for.

The ADD gate at 78,160.0 fired and confirmed cleanly: one bar closed 78,192.3, the next closed 78,194.6, and price has run 486 points further to 78,819.8. Acting on the add was right.

There is a correction to make to last session's lesson. That session proposed grading gates on the *sequence* of triggers and treating a voided first trigger as an early probe. Applied here it would have produced a bad trade. The difference is depth. On 28 August the second trigger ran 1,070 points through the level and was correct; here it clipped the line by five points and was noise. So the rule gets a qualifier — a repeated trigger means something only when the later one penetrates *further* than the first. Shallower second triggers are the level being defended, not broken.
