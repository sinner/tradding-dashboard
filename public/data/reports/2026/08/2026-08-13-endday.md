## Bias review

Midday published two levels and one trade. The levels both held their definitions and neither fired. The trade lost.

The lowest print since was 63,115.0 on the 18:30Z bar and the lowest settled close was 63,146.4, so the line was missed by 268.7 points on prints and 300.1 on settles. It was never in play. The add line sat at 63,625.2, the 1H EMA20 at the time, and needed a settled close above it with open interest not rising. Price reached 63,645.4 on the 22:15Z bar, 20.2 points through the level, but the 1H bar containing that spike settled 63,531.6. The settle condition withheld the signal and spared a long that would have been bought at the high of the day. That is the composite trigger doing precisely the job it was built for, and it is the second consecutive session where the settle requirement has been worth more than the level itself.

The trade is the negative. Midday opened a 60 USDT futures short at 2x from 63,273.4 with the stop at 63,580, placed above the 16:00Z bar high and 1.06 hourly ATRs away. The 22:15Z bar traded 63,645.4 and it was stopped, for roughly 29 cents. The reasoning behind it was internally sound — the range floor had broken on a genuine settle, every 1H average was overhead, the 1H MACD had just crossed negative, the daily MACD had gone below zero for the first time this month, and realised long liquidations were running at nearly three times shorts. It was still the wrong trade, and the reason is worth stating narrowly rather than as "do not short". Entering a continuation at the retest of a level that has just broken on a cascade puts you exactly where the next fuel is standing. The tape settled the argument: 591,179 dollars of *short* liquidations printed between 17:49Z and 22:16Z, the largest single fill 382.33 contracts at 63,476.3, two hundred points above the entry. The desk was on the same side as the crowd that got run over.

One thing was right and should be banked. Midday refused to sell the spot core into the low of the flush at 63,273.4. Spot is 245.7 points higher.

## Key risks

The honest asymmetry is that the constructive signals are all fast-timeframe and the bearish ones are all structural. A positive 1H MACD histogram and a 4H hammer can be unwound in two bars; four daily averages overhead and a 1.88 long/short ratio cannot. If the reclaim fails while crowding is this high, the downside path is already carved. Conversely, the risk to the bearish tilt is equally specific — a settled close above 63,672 with open interest falling would be the first real evidence in three sessions that someone is buying rather than covering, and this report would be on the wrong side of it.
