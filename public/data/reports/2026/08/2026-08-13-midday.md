## Bias review

Morning published two levels and both of them fired, in the worst possible order. The add line at 63,800 fired first, on fully valid mechanics — the 14:00Z hourly bar settled 63,862.0, sixty-two points above the line, with aggregate open interest falling from $2,688,568,878 to $2,679,314,772 across that same bar, which satisfied both halves of the composite trigger morning had written. Two bars later price traded 62,846.3. Then the reduce line at 63,309.4 fired on the 16:00Z bar, a genuine settle at 63,165.1, a hundred and forty-four points through it.

A desk that obeyed both triggers mechanically would have bought at 63,862.0 and sold at 63,165.1 inside two hours, for minus six hundred and ninety-seven points. That is the lesson worth keeping from today and it is not a lesson about direction. It is that composite triggers firing on a spike into a level, inside a range that is actively breaking, produce the worst fills available. The trigger was well-constructed and it still lost money, because the market was in the process of failing while it printed.

The trade itself was right, and it is the first winner in six sessions. Morning trimmed seventeen percent of the spot bag at 63,476.3 and the mark is 63,273.4, so that trim sits two hundred and three points in front, up 0.32% on the trimmed notional. Worth recording honestly: it was underwater by 523.6 points at the 63,999.9 session high before it worked. Anyone managing it tightly would have been stopped out of a trade that ended up correct. Rolling record moves to two of the last nine, and the pattern in those two is that both winners were reductions while all four losers were adds.
