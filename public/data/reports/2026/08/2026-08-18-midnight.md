## Bias review

Endday published bullish at 6.75 with spot at 64,440.0. Acting on the reduce would have hurt. A sale at roughly 64,225.2 marks against spot 64,340.1 — about 114.9 points, 0.18% of the reduced size, given away. Endday's resting stop at 63,979.7 was never touched: the low of the window was 64,039.0, a clear 59.3 points above it. The overnight protection cost nothing and did nothing, which is exactly what a stop should do when it isn't needed.

Two other endday claims deserve honest marking. It reported the 1H ema50 crossing above the 1H ema200 for the first time in the sequence; on this run's 299-bar recomputation ema50 is 63,756.1 and ema200 is 63,778.3, so that cross is not present. And it called the daily ema50 reclaim "the single new structural fact of this session" — the daily ema50 now measures 64,361.8 and spot sits 21.7 points beneath it. The fact lasted seven hours.

That makes this the first NO after four consecutive helping sessions, the longest positive run in the file. It is worth being precise about why: the desk did not misread the tape, it published a rule and the rule was wrong. A level broken on settled closes and reclaimed within three hours is evidence of strength. A mechanical reduce trigger cannot tell the difference between a breakdown and a shakeout, and this one couldn't either.
