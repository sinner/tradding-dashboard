## Bias review

Midday published a bearish call at 63,988.7 with probabilities of 18 / 38 / 44 and a brand-new reduce line at 63,750. Both halves of that call get a mixed grade.

The reduce line was right to be placed where it was, and it did not fire. Price probed 63,832.3 at 17:00Z and 63,818.1 at 18:30Z, stopping 68.1 points short of the trigger on the closer attempt, and the lowest settled hourly close of the whole stretch was 63,892.9. The 4H EMA200 at 63,769.94 — the number midday called the single most important on the board — was never touched. Two tests, two holds.

The bearish tilt itself was too heavy. Midday assigned 44% to bearish and the market went sideways for seven hours in a 386.2-point box. Not wrong, but not paid either.

The one forward-looking check midday named as decisive came back badly. It said explicitly that the thing to watch tonight was whether Monday printed the first ETF outflow of the streak. It did.

Acting helped: essentially not at all, and honestly so. The midday trim sold 0.00055722 BTC at 63,988.7 and spot is now 63,986.0 — the trade is ahead by 2.7 points, about a tenth of a cent. What the trim actually bought was optionality, and that gets judged overnight, not on a 2.7-point mark.

## What changed

Four things, and they do not point the same way.

Open interest fell another 0.58% to $2,040,525,808, extending the morning's 0.69% decline. Two consecutive sessions of shrinking open interest is a deleveraging, and deleveragings tend to want one more flush before they finish.

The long/short account ratio rose again — 1.23 this morning, 1.45 at midday, 1.62 now — across a session in which price fell 1,279 points from the day's high. Accounts are averaging down, not capitulating. That is what keeps the stop shelf below restocked.

Against that, forced selling essentially stopped. In the six hours before the midday snapshot the market liquidated $10,377,727 of longs. In the seven hours since, $1,134,318 against $8,925 of shorts — a 92% collapse in the rate.

And the ETF streak broke. More on that below.
