The finding underneath that is the interesting part, and it vindicates a call that looked eccentric when it was made. Midnight placed its reduce gate 600.7 points out — 2.54 hourly ATRs, far outside the 0.4-to-0.6 band every gate in this log has used — and justified the width by arguing that a collapsed ATR of 236.57 made the standard band too tight to survive ordinary noise. That reasoning was right, and it is the only reason today produced a clean signal instead of a whipsaw. A 0.5-ATR gate would have sat near 78,562 and been triggered by the perfectly ordinary 20:00Z close of 78,591.4, hours before anything actually broke. The rule to carry forward: when hourly ATR has contracted more than 50 percent from the prior session, abandon the band and put the gate on the structural shelf, however many ATRs out that lands.

The second finding is a negative result worth as much as a positive one. The divergence check has been standing procedure since 27 August endday. It ran tonight and came back clean — the 79,401.4 high carried 1H RSI 76.59 against 69.49 at the prior 78,852.8 high, so momentum confirmed the new high rather than diverging from it. The reversal came anyway. What was available was not divergence but altitude: 76.59 is above the 75 threshold this desk treats as overbought in crypto, it printed on the exact bar that made the high, and the very next bar started the slide. A confirmed high can still be an exhausted one. Both checks belong in the routine now, not just the first.

## Liquidation map — where the magnets are

This is the second session running with measured data rather than leverage-bracket estimates, and it is the best data this log has had.

Different hour, much smaller window, same signature. That is why the map is corroborated rather than merely inherited.

The shape is lopsided and it matters. Below the market: 764,091 USD at the 77,450.0 shelf, 346,610 USD at 77,155.3, and 1,048,285 USD at the 77,044.5 magnet, which took that in a two-second span. Above the market: 145,556 USD across two buckets. Fourteen times more fuel below than above. Forced long selling outweighed forced short covering 9.35 to one.

The caveat is the same one as last session and it deserves repeating: these are realized fills, not resting orders. They mark where leverage has been cleared, not where it is still waiting. A pool that has already absorbed a flush is partly spent — which is exactly why the bias is range with a tilt rather than outright bearish.
